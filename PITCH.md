## Before 4:15

In this order. Stop when you run out of time.

1. Do the warm-up run in [DEMO.md](DEMO.md#pre-demo-checklist) (reset, Stripe OA, all five judge emails).
2. Open each judge's prep doc and find the **one line to point at** from the table below. Know where it is on the page.
3. Click **Quiz me** on one judge's prep doc and time the first question. Under 8 seconds: use it live. Slower: skip it.
4. Say the opener and the closer out loud twice. Those two lines are the ones that have to land.

## The flow (75 seconds)

Same five beats for every judge. Only beats 1, 3 and 4 change per judge.

| # | Beat | Time | What happens | Line |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Hook | 10s | Nothing on screen yet. Eye contact. | Opener joke, then: "It reads my recruiting email and does the part I keep skipping." |
| 2 | OA lands | 20s | Send the Stripe OA. Card goes Working, then Scheduled. Read the *why* line. | "Claude picked the slot around my classes and my bCourses deadlines. Code then checks the slot is really free." |
| 3 | Judge move | 20s | Ask their name. Send their email. Open **Read prep doc**. Point at their one line. | "This is what I would read 20 minutes before interviewing with you." |
| 4 | One extra | 15s | Pick ONE by judge type (below). | See per-judge table. |
| 5 | Close | 10s | Hands off the laptop. | "It never sends email. Every block shows its reasoning. I just show up." Then the closer joke. |

**The one extra (beat 4). Pick by who is in front of you:**

- **Builder judge (Jay, Ye):** click **Move this block** on the OA card. It re-picks and re-verifies in one click.
  Say: "Claude proposes, code verifies. If Claude picks a slot that overlaps a class, code rejects it."
- **Investor judge (Ajay, Ashtan):** click **Quiz me** on their prep doc. First question appears in their voice.
  Say: "And then it interviews me as you." Do not answer the question. Let it sit, move to the close.
- **Student founder (Ayush):** point at the **This week** strip: classes, bCourses deadlines, and the orange blocks.
  Say: "It knows I have a midterm Thursday, so it did not put the OA on Wednesday night."

If a queue is forming, drop beat 4. Never drop beat 3.

## Per-judge playbook

One joke per judge, maximum. The joke is always about Victor, never about them.

### Jay Patel, VoiceBit (co-founder, CTO)

- **Cares about:** things that work in messy real conditions. Metrics over hype.
- **Point at in his doc:** the line about VoiceBit's first AI pizza order missing the toppings (he told that story on a podcast, it is in "Your interviewer").
- **Say:** "Your first AI pizza order forgot the toppings. Our version of that is booking my OA in the middle of a midterm. So code checks every slot Claude picks, and rejects it if it overlaps anything."
- **Borrow his framing:** "You said restaurants do not buy AI, they buy recovered revenue. Students do not buy AI either. They buy not getting dropped by Stripe for missing a deadline."
- **Extra beat:** Move this block. If he digs in, mention the tricky samples (rejection email, job board digest, a deadline given in Eastern time) and that repeats show as a Reminder, not a double booking.
- **Do not:** quote funding or headcount, or say "VoiceBite" (different company).

### Ajay Kamat, Pear VC (general partner)

- **Cares about:** founder speed, honesty about traction, founders who are skeptical of their own product.
- **Point at in his doc:** the Watch-outs line. The recruiter email says "Partner"; the doc noticed he was promoted to general partner in October.
- **Say:** "The email I got says Partner. The doc caught that you were promoted last month. Congratulations, by the way."
- **Then be blunt before he asks:** "Users: three. Us. Built today. Nobody pays yet."
- **Joke:** "PearX takes under one percent. I miss deadlines for places that take ten. I needed the help."
- **Extra beat:** Quiz me. Then the buyer answer: student pays a few dollars a month in recruiting season, or a career center buys it. Untested, say so.
- **Do not:** pitch market size numbers you cannot source.

### Ashtan Jordan, Precursor Ventures (principal)

- **Cares about:** people over product. Precursor does not screen founders by school or employer.
- **Point at in the doc:** the "people over product" line at the top, and that every fact has a numbered source.
- **Say:** "Precursor invests in people over product. Good news: this product only exists because the person is flawed."
- **Tell the founder story, not the feature list:** this is Victor's own problem, three people built it in a day, and each owned a part (Gmail and Calendar, the classifier and scheduler, the research and demo).
- **Extra beat:** Quiz me. "And then it interviews me as you."
- **Do not:** claim investing experience. Refer to Ashtan by name; the doc does not state pronouns, so do not guess.

### Ye Wang, EverCurrent (founder, CEO)

- **Cares about:** strong algorithms, AI that is grounded and checkable, owning work end to end. IOI and MIT background, so expect a deep follow-up.
- **Point at in her doc:** the Watch-outs line where it refuses to confuse EverCurrent with a defunct "Evercurrent" in Austin.
- **Say:** "It found a second Evercurrent, a dead one in Austin, and flagged it so I would not walk in talking about the wrong company. When it cannot identify someone, it says so and does not guess."
- **Joke:** "You build schedule safeguards for hardware teams. This is a schedule safeguard for one unreliable piece of hardware. Me."
- **Extra beat:** Move this block, with the split stated exactly: the model proposes a slot and explains it; deterministic code checks overlap and the 9 AM to 10 PM window, asks once more on failure, then falls back to the earliest free slot.
- **Do not:** hand-wave. If she asks something you have not built, say "we have not built that."

### Ayush Malhotra, Talkhappi (co-founder, Berkeley student)

- **Cares about:** research and measurable outcomes. He is on this campus and knows bCourses.
- **Point at in his doc:** "so you share a campus" at the top, and the line admitting it found no recent news and telling Victor to ask what is current.
- **Say:** "It could not find recent news on Talkhappi, so it told me to ask you, which is better than making something up."
- **Joke:** "You know bCourses. This reads it and schedules around my midterms, which is more than I do."
- **Be honest on measurement, unprompted:** "We have a check script over a handful of tricky emails. We do not have accuracy numbers. That is the first thing we would measure."
- **Extra beat:** the This week strip.
- **Do not:** call him CFO. Say "co-founder."

## Joke bank

Deadpan. Say it like a fact, do not wait for the laugh, keep moving. Use at most two per table:
one opener, one closer.

**Openers (pick one)**

1. "It is called Hire Me Maybe. The 'maybe' is entirely my fault."
2. "I can pass an online assessment. What I cannot do is open the email."
3. "I have lost more jobs to my calendar than to LeetCode."
4. "My recruiting process was: see email, feel stress, close laptop. We automated the first step and removed the other two."

**Mid-demo (only if the room is warm)**

5. While the card says Working: "This is the longest anyone has thought about my schedule, including me."
6. On the prep doc: "Twenty minutes of prep. That is twenty more than my current record."
7. On "Did you take it?" (the follow-up ping after an OA block ends): "It also checks afterwards whether I actually did it. We accidentally built my mom."
8. On never sending email: "It cannot send email. I asked for that. Nothing with my name on it should send email at 2 AM, including me."

**Closers (pick one)**

9. "It reads the email, picks the time and writes the prep. My only remaining job is to show up. We are working on that."
10. To a judge who is hiring: "If you do want to hire me, maybe, just send an email. It will be on my calendar before you put your phone down."
11. If the prize includes interviews with the judges (it did last year; confirm for today): "I hear the prize is an interview with you. The prep doc is already done, so that would save us both some time."

**If something breaks**

12. "That is the app staying on brand. It also left this to the last minute." Then switch to the warm-up cards.

**Leave out**

- "Chud", "cooked", "locked in" and other slang. It reads as funny among friends and as unserious to an investor.
- Anything about the judge: their age, company size, a failed startup, a title mix-up.
- Jokes about AI replacing recruiters or cheating on OAs. The app schedules and preps. It does not take the test.
- Three jokes in a row. After a laugh, the next sentence must be a fact about the product.

## Delivery rules

- Problem first, always. The opener joke replaces nothing; the problem sentence follows it.
- Say one honest limit before they ask. "Yours is cached, anyone else takes twenty seconds live" is the best one.
- Read the *why* line on the card out loud. It is the proof Claude reasoned, and judges will not read it themselves.
- Ask the judge one question from their doc's "Questions to ask them" section if there is time left. It shows the doc is useful, and it gets them talking.
- When you finish, stop talking. Let them ask.
