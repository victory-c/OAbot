# Talkhappi: Software Engineering Intern interview prep

## The 60-second version
- Talkhappi is a mental-wellness startup. Its site calls the product "The first AI-based therapist designed to help you" [[1]](https://talkhappi.ca/). Users talk to it and get scored using a "Happi score" on how "happy" or verbally positive they were talking [[2]](https://www.youthcreativityfund.ca/success-stories/talkhappi).
- You're meeting Ayush Malhotra, a co-founder. His LinkedIn headline lists Econ & Data Science at UC Berkeley [[3]](https://www.linkedin.com/in/ayush-malhotra-4416b51a5/?_l=en), so you share a campus.
- Your best angle: you make AI output trustworthy. That means your source-verification fixes at Quest Space and your evals with human-review routing. Trust matters a lot in a therapy-style product.

## The company right now
I found no recent news, funding or launches. Everything below is from 2021 to 2023 or undated, so ask what's current.
- Talkhappi was invited to attend the 2023 SXSW EDU conference in Austin, Texas as a finalist (sole international finalist), with a Youth Creativity Fund micro-grant covering some travel costs [[2]](https://www.youthcreativityfund.ca/success-stories/talkhappi).
- The site claims its AI has an accuracy of over 90% for identifying your situation [[1]](https://talkhappi.ca/).
- On its origins, the site says: "We formed during the quarantine where we recognized the problem people faced with isolation and no one to talk to." [[1]](https://talkhappi.ca/)

## Your interviewer(s)
**Ayush Malhotra**
- His LinkedIn profile lists him as Co-Founder of TalkhAPPi, with a headline mentioning Econ & DS @ UC Berkeley, and the co-founder role starting Oct 2020 [[3]](https://www.linkedin.com/in/ayush-malhotra-4416b51a5/?_l=en).

- His LinkedIn profile refers to research with the University of Waterloo on the effects of increased verbal positivity, through a novel wellness tool, on students' academic performance [[3]](https://www.linkedin.com/in/ayush-malhotra-4416b51a5/?_l=en).
- The company site lists him on the team as Chief Financial Officer [[1]](https://talkhappi.ca/).

What this suggests: expect interest in research and measurable outcomes, not only engineering.

## What they will probably ask
1. **"Why Talkhappi?"** Connect your CogSci coursework (CogSci 131) and your Algoverse LLM-bias research to measuring behavior with AI.
2. **"How would you make our AI safer or more accurate?"** Use the storefront harness: a contract gate plus an LLM-judge rubric, with unclear cases routed to a human.
3. **"Tell me about shipping fast on a real codebase."** Quest Space: 42 PRs in about six weeks, plus 12 P0 reliability fixes before a deadline.
4. **"How do you handle ambiguity?"** Your "ask-unless-sure" policy for unclear file references, and how you traced the Drive bug.

## Questions to ask them
- "How do you validate the Happi score and the 90% accuracy figure today? Could an intern help build an eval for it?"
- "Does your verbal-positivity research shape the roadmap?"
- "What does the stack and the AI model layer look like today?"

## Watch-outs
- The company site lists his title as Chief Financial Officer [[1]](https://talkhappi.ca/), while the recruiter's email and his LinkedIn say co-founder. Just say "co-founder."
- Don't mix them up with "talkappi," an unrelated Japanese AI chatbot product for tourism and hotels [[5]](https://talkappi.com/).
- Your strongest stack is Python. Be honest about your TypeScript level if they ask about web development.
